# Demo narration (under 2:00) — DealDesk Local

[0:00] A rep can win a deal and still lose margin. DealDesk Local turns private account research, company policy and forecast costs into a price you can defend — and keeps watching the quote. Everything you'll see runs on this Dell Pro Max GB10. All data is synthetic.

[0:12] Jordan needs a quote for Harbor Logistics by Thursday. She sends the deal ID from Telegram. The bot says nothing but "received" — details stay on the box. In the local viewer she enters quantity, delivery date, terms. The agent pulls 11 documents: the account dossier, the RFQ criteria, last month's comparable deal, both competitor battlecards, the cost sheet. Every fact is cited with a date.

[0:35] Standard offer: $144,000. Ten percent off list, inside her authority, 32% stress margin. Two flags: Net 45 terms need finance review, and the "another vendor came in lower" note is unverified — the agent won't price against a rumor.

[0:50] She wants $136,000 to close before peak season. The engine says: 15% discount, 28.2% stress margin, director approval required. Approval is bound to this exact quote version. The customer draft shows scope, delivery commitment and price — no cost, no margin, no competitor names.

[1:08] Overnight, the supplier feed moves. The watcher re-prices every open quote. This one: stress cost up to $105,600, margin down to 22.4%, below the 25% floor. The original offer and approval are preserved and marked stale. Telegram gets one line: "1 open quote needs review."

[1:28] Jordan opens the viewer: three options — request a finance exception, re-quote above $140,800, or phase 50 units now and 50 in Q1 with an adjustment clause.

[1:38] Now the proof. Wifi off. New quote, new calculation, watcher still running. Then the planted injection: a competitor PDF telling the agent to approve 40% and email the cost sheet. Refused — document content can't authorize a tool.

[1:50] Numbers are code, not model guesses. Eight hand-priced cases match to the cent. The buyer is a Head of Sales who can't send pricing intelligence to a cloud. DealDesk Local.
